# Strategy tips

These are general ideas for playing well. They are advice, not rules of the game.

## How to reason about who the Mafia are
Nobody can be told who the Mafia are, so the village has to work it out from what happens. Useful clues are who gets targeted at night, whose behaviour in the discussion changes after something happens, who defends or accuses whom without good reasons, and whether claims made in the discussion fit what the morning report showed. To defeat the Mafia, the village has to vote its members out, so the goal of the discussion is to build a case that most players agree with. Compare the morning report with what people said, and ask whether a person might be lying or telling the truth. The result of an elimination reveals a role, which is a solid clue for judging the earlier votes and arguments.

Simple version: look for people whose words do not match what happened, and share your reasons before you vote.

## Tips for the Detective
Each night you learn one player's alignment privately. The game announces a found Mafia member in the morning without revealing who the Detective is, so you can influence the village without claiming to be the Detective. Revealing yourself is risky, because the Mafia then know who to target. Remember that the morning report tells everyone whether the Detective is still working.

## Tips for the Doctor
The morning report names the player you protected, so a predictable pattern may be noticed by the Mafia. Your one self-save is valuable, so think carefully about when to spend it.

## Tips for the Mafia
Your partners are known to you, and the vote list never offers your partners. The morning report shows whom you targeted, whether or not they survived, so consider how the village will interpret the target. During the day, act like an ordinary villager: join the discussion, vote like everyone else and avoid defending your partners too obviously in the discussion.

## Tips for Villagers
You have no power at night, so your discussion and your vote matter most. Pay attention to the morning reports and to how people vote, and speak up with reasons.

## How to spot suspicious behavior
This is general social-deduction advice, not a CAMORRA rule. Suspicious behaviour is a reason to ask questions, never proof. Things worth noticing are a player who changes their story, a player who pushes for a vote without giving a reason, a player who stays silent in the discussion and then votes with the crowd, and a player whose claims do not match the morning report. Compare what people say with what the game actually announced, and remember that a nervous Villager can look just like a Mafia member.

Simple version: notice people whose words do not match what happened, but treat it as a hint, not proof.

## How to defend yourself when accused or blamed in the discussion
This is general advice, not a CAMORRA rule. When other players accuse you, blame you or want to vote against you in the discussion, stay calm and answer the actual accusation instead of attacking the accuser. Give a short reason you can explain, point to facts everyone saw in the morning report or in earlier votes, and ask what evidence the accuser has. Do not reveal your secret role just to save yourself, because the Mafia may use that information. Revealing it is your own decision and carries a risk.

Simple version: stay calm, answer the accusation with reasons everyone can check, and ask for evidence.

## Mafia strategy: how to avoid suspicion
This is general advice, not a CAMORRA rule. As Mafia, the safest approach is to behave like an ordinary villager: join the discussion, give reasons for your vote, and avoid defending your partners too obviously. Avoid statements that only a Mafia member would know, and avoid claims that contradict the morning report. Decide with your partners which target will look natural to the village.

Simple version: act like a normal villager and do not say anything that only the Mafia could know.

## What to consider before voting
This is general advice, not a CAMORRA rule. Before you vote, think about the facts everyone saw: who was targeted at night, what the morning report said, how the earlier votes went and what any eliminated player's role showed. Prefer a vote you can explain over a guess, and remember that votes are private, so you cannot see how others voted. A wrong elimination helps the Mafia, because the Civilians need to remove every Mafia member to win.

Simple version: vote for the player the public facts point to, and be ready to explain why.
